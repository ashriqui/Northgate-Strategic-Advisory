The Standing Invitation

What Ottawa's New Drone Program Gets Right

Defence Procurement Government Contracts Small Business

Most government procurement runs like a train station. There's a schedule, a departure time, and if you miss it, you're waiting for the next one, sometimes years down the track. On July 23rd, Ottawa launched something that runs more like a subway line: miss this one, catch the next.

The Defence Drone Initiative, led jointly by the Department of National Defence and the new Defence Investment Agency, is built to move Canadian drone and uncrewed-systems technology from concept to testing to production faster than the old system managed. It covers a wide stretch of ground: tactical surveillance drones, uncrewed ground vehicles for logistics, maritime systems, counter-drone interceptors, even precision strike systems. The government is explicit about who it wants applying, naming start-ups and medium-sized enterprises right alongside the established defence primes, not as an afterthought but as the point.

The application window, technically, closes August 14th. A supplier info session runs July 30th for anyone who wants to understand the fine print before applying. But the part worth sitting with is what happens after that date passes. Ottawa says it expects to run periodic updates to the arrangement, letting new suppliers apply and qualify well after this first round closes. Qualifying doesn't guarantee a contract, it gets you into position to compete for testing, prototyping, and eventually production work as it comes up. Miss the first train and there's a reasonable chance you're standing on the platform for the next one within months, not years.

That's a real departure from how this kind of thing usually works, and it says as much about the framework as it does about drones specifically. Doug Guzman, the Defence Investment Agency's CEO, described the goal as building Canada's uncrewed systems ecosystem, not just acquiring new equipment. An ecosystem needs new arrivals on a rolling basis, which is a very different design decision than a single deadline for a single fixed pool of winners.

For a small or mid-sized Canadian manufacturer with something to offer here, robotics, sensors, secure communications, advanced materials, this is a door that's staying open rather than closing behind the first group through it. The company that's ready in August gets the head start. The company that gets ready in October still gets on the train.
